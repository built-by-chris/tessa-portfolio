## About this work

This senior capstone places modern health-policy debates within a longer constitutional and political-development framework. It connects competing interpretations of the General Welfare Clause, federalism, fiscal capacity, and the historical growth of public health programs to contemporary proposals for single-payer health care.

Rather than treating health reform as a stand-alone policy dispute, the paper asks how American institutional design, political culture, and the historical division of federal and state authority shape what reforms are possible and how they might function.
